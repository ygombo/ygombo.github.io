Keeping robot teams in formation with Delayed Self-Reinforcement

When a team of robots carries a flexible object, only the leader knows where to go. Everyone else follows by reacting to their neighbours, and that lag lets the object stretch and bend. DSR lets each robot also reinforce its own most recent motion, using information it already has, so the whole team moves together without extra communication.

Read the paper ↗

How it works

Same robots, same target, two ways of following.

1

The setup

A chain of robots holds a flexible object. The leader is told the target position; followers only sense their immediate neighbours.

2

Standard approach

Each robot moves toward its neighbours. The command trickles down the chain slowly, so the object gets stretched while the team catches up.

3

With DSR

Each robot adds a term based on its own previous update. Information effectively travels faster, so the team moves as a unit with far less deformation.

The math behind it
StandardY[m+1] = (I − γ δt K) Y[m] + γ δt B yd[m]
DSRY[m+1] = Y[m] − αβ δt K Y[m] + αβ δt B yd[m] + (I − βK)(Y[m] − Y[m−1])
  • Y stacks every robot's position; K is the pinned graph Laplacian (neighbour stiffness s plus leader pin kₚ); B feeds the target yd to the leaders.
  • The extra DSR term (I − βK)(Y[m] − Y[m−1]) reuses each robot's last update, so no additional communication is needed.
  • Stability needs 0 < γ·δt < 2/λmax(K) for standard, and β < 4 / (λmax(K)(α·δt + 2)) for DSR.
