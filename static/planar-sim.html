Carrying a shape around corners with DSR

Two identical robot teams surround a circular object and follow the same leader along a randomly wandering path. Every time the leader turns, the followers have to catch up. Watch how the standard team squashes the object, while the DSR team keeps it close to a circle.

See the 1D version Read the paper ↗

How it works

Same robots, same path, two ways of following.

1

The setup

Robots form a ring around a circular object. Only the leader is pulled toward a point moving along the path; every other robot just responds to its two neighbours.

2

Standard approach

A turn by the leader diffuses slowly around the ring. Robots far from the leader lag behind, so the object gets dragged into an oval.

3

With DSR

Each robot also reuses its own previous motion. The leader's change spreads around the ring much faster, so the team turns together and the shape holds.

The math behind it

The same update laws as the 1D simulation, applied separately to each robot's x and y position on a ring network.

StandardY[m+1] = Y[m] + γ δt (−K Y[m] + B yd[m])
DSRY[m+1] = Y[m] + αβ δt (−K Y[m] + B yd[m]) + (I − βK)(Y[m] − Y[m−1])
  • K is the ring Laplacian (neighbour stiffness) with the leader pinned to the moving path point yd; δt = 0.03 s.
  • Deformation is the RMS distance of the robots from their own centroid, divided by the object radius. 0% is a perfect circle.
  • This is an illustrative kinematic model. It does not simulate material forces or collisions.
